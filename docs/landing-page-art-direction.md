# ShipWright Landing Page Art Direction

## Purpose
This document translates the ShipWright brand world into landing-page composition, visual storytelling, and implementation direction. It should guide future landing-page phases before more component work is started.

The landing page should create desire and confidence. It should not merely explain product features.

## Strategic Landing Page Role
The public landing page must make ShipWright feel like:

- A platform company, not a single dashboard.
- Premium logistics infrastructure, not delivery-app tooling.
- A proof-led operating system for local commerce.
- An assistive command centre with human approval, not autonomous AI theatre.

## Page-Level Emotional Arc
The page should move through five emotional states:

1. Recognition: this is infrastructure for local commerce in motion.
2. Atmosphere: the operating world feels cinematic, warm, and premium.
3. Clarity: ShipWright connects ordering, dispatch, courier execution, tracking, payments, and proof.
4. Confidence: command intelligence and proof artifacts reduce operational risk.
5. Invitation: controlled pilots and demos feel credible, not salesy.

## Composition System
### Hero
The hero should not be only large typography. It should balance copy with an art-directed operational scene.

Preferred structure:

- Left or staggered editorial copy.
- Large right-side or full-bleed visual scene.
- Route-light movement as the composition spine.
- Floating signal fragments for command, proof, and risk.
- One clear primary CTA and one secondary proof/demo CTA.

The hero should feel like: "the operating system for modern local commerce."

### Editorial Sections
Editorial sections should be spacious and narrative-led.

Use:

- Large white or warm off-white spaces.
- One idea per section.
- Strong section openers.
- Supporting proof or visual rhythm.

Avoid:

- Repeated three-card grids.
- Dashboard screenshot walls.
- Dense text stacks.
- Equal-weight feature blocks.

### Visual Interruption Moments
The page needs memorable pauses between explanation sections.

Potential moments:

- A cinematic route-light statement section.
- A dark but sparse command intelligence scene.
- A proof closeout moment with green evidence markers.
- A commerce movement scene with warm urban lighting.
- An oversized quote or operating principle.

### Platform Ecosystem Sections
Platform depth should be communicated through orchestration, not a long list of features.

Frame the ecosystem as:

- Order intake.
- Dispatch coordination.
- Courier execution.
- Tracking and proof.
- Payment-state visibility.
- Command intelligence.
- Admin oversight.
- Pilot closeout.

Each pillar should explain its role in the operating loop.

## Hero Exploration Concepts
### Concept 1: Route Orchestration
Visual composition:

- Abstract city grid in perspective.
- Warm order node entering from the lower left.
- Blue command node in the centre.
- Green proof node near the destination.
- Floating cards show "Order authorised", "Courier matched", and "Proof recorded".

Emotional approach:

- Infrastructure confidence.
- Movement with control.
- Premium city logistics.

Best for:

- Investor and platform positioning.

### Concept 2: Commerce Movement
Visual composition:

- Merchant/kitchen light, courier trail, customer doorstep marker.
- More warmth, softer human commercial tone.
- Less platform geometry, more fulfilment flow.

Emotional approach:

- Local commerce feels alive.
- Operators keep service moving.
- Human warmth without cartoon illustration.

Best for:

- Restaurants, local retailers, and pilot merchants.

### Concept 3: Calm Command Centre
Visual composition:

- Sparse command surface fragments in a cinematic space.
- Daily briefing, payment risk, job state, and proof signal floating around a route spine.
- Mostly white/off-white with controlled blue command light.

Emotional approach:

- Calm control.
- Serious operations software.
- Intelligence that supports human decision-making.

Best for:

- Operator and command intelligence messaging.

### Concept 4: City-Grid Intelligence
Visual composition:

- Abstract city blocks from an elevated angle.
- Route light moves through the grid.
- Signal nodes represent merchant, courier, customer, and platform oversight.

Emotional approach:

- Urban infrastructure.
- Sophisticated local logistics.
- Scaled orchestration.

Best for:

- Platform-company maturity and ecosystem navigation.

### Concept 5: Fulfilment Lifecycle
Visual composition:

- A horizontal cinematic sequence: order, authorisation, dispatch, pickup, delivery, proof, report.
- Each stage is a small art-directed marker rather than a UI card.

Emotional approach:

- Trust through sequence.
- Proof-led execution.
- No loose ends.

Best for:

- Demo conversion and controlled-pilot explanation.

## Illustration Direction
Future illustration should be:

- Dimensional SVG or lightweight vector.
- Layered and atmospheric.
- Abstract enough to avoid literal fake tracking.
- Specific enough to feel proprietary to ShipWright.
- Built around route-light, command nodes, proof markers, and urban grid cues.

Preferred techniques:

- SVG paths for route trails.
- Soft radial lighting inside the asset, not page-wide blobs.
- Subtle perspective grids.
- Small floating signal panels.
- Clean labels where they add clarity.
- Proof ticks, closeout stamps, and handoff markers.

Avoid:

- Generic SaaS vector people.
- Clip-art couriers.
- Cartoon scooters.
- Dense product screenshots.
- 3D mockups that feel unrelated to logistics.
- Neon cyber-city aesthetics.

## Colour Philosophy
### Commerce Amber
Use for:

- Order entry.
- Merchant activity.
- Customer checkout energy.
- Warm city-light moments.

Restraint:

- Should feel like warm operational light, not orange decoration.

### Command Blue
Use for:

- Command Intelligence.
- Operator review.
- Dispatch coordination.
- Admin oversight.

Restraint:

- Should feel calm and precise, not corporate default blue.

### Proof Green
Use for:

- POD.
- Capture.
- Fulfilled state.
- Closeout evidence.

Restraint:

- Should feel like completion and trust, not a bright success alert.

### Atmospheric Navy
Use for:

- Rare cinematic dark moments.
- Footer atmosphere.
- Oversight and infrastructure depth.

Restraint:

- Dark sections must be sparse and elegant. They should never become a loud dashboard wall.

### Rose/Violet Ambient Tone
Use for:

- Secondary atmospheric depth only.

Restraint:

- Never make ShipWright feel purple-led.

## Typography Philosophy
Current risk: typography can become too heavy and dominate the page.

Rules:

- Hero typography may be large, but should not overpower the visual scene.
- Section headings should vary in scale and rhythm.
- Use 600-700 weight for major headings, 500-600 for section/subsection titles.
- Supporting copy should be elegant, generous, and easy to read.
- Avoid repeated giant headings in every section.
- Typography should lead when the section is editorial; it should support when the section is visual.

Spacing:

- Increase vertical rhythm between major sections.
- Keep line lengths restrained.
- Use whitespace as a premium signal.
- Avoid compressed marketing density.

## Motion Philosophy
Motion is not a dependency for this phase, but future motion should follow these rules:

- Route-light may gently draw or pulse to imply movement.
- Floating cards may lift subtly on hover.
- Mega menus may fade/slide softly.
- Section reveals should be slow enough to feel premium.
- Motion should be directional and operational, not playful.
- No bouncy easing.
- No heavy animation libraries unless a later phase proves the value.
- Respect reduced-motion settings.

## Mega Menu Philosophy
Mega menus should communicate platform maturity and ecosystem depth.

They should feel:

- Luxurious but usable.
- Soft and layered.
- Spacious.
- Editorial, not enterprise-cluttered.
- Connected to the route-light visual system.

Mega menu visual anchors should:

- Use small route scenes.
- Include commerce, command, and proof nodes.
- Reinforce the section theme.
- Avoid becoming mini dashboards.

Interaction:

- Hover/focus should feel calm and deliberate.
- Keyboard access must remain intact.
- Mobile nav should simplify rather than force desktop mega-menu behavior.

## Implementation Notes For Later Phases
- Build 2-4 more SVG-native scene assets before another major landing redesign.
- Decide whether the hero should use one large asset or a sequence of composable SVG layers.
- Consider a small proprietary marker/icon set for commerce, command, courier, proof, and oversight.
- Add motion only after static art direction is strong.
- Keep all marketing atmosphere isolated from internal app command surfaces.

## Open Creative Questions
- Should ShipWright commission bespoke illustration assets or continue building SVG-native compositions in-repo?
- Should the public site include more human presence, or stay abstract and infrastructure-led?
- Which hero concept should become the canonical brand direction?
- Should demo conversion pages share the same art world or stay more functional?
- How cinematic can the landing page become before it feels less like serious software?
